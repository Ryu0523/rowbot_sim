## 1. Task and error-estimation target
**Left**
- 5.4 m planing USV with waterjet; goal: maximum speed along track under safety limits (impact acceleration, bow clearance, yaw rate, track deviation)
- Sampling MPC: every 0.24 s, 128 thrust plans rolled out 5.8 s ahead with a reduced model (calm water, 10 states, no roll)
- Target: one-step error = (true next state − reduced-model prediction from the same state and command) / 0.24 s, five velocity channels
- A learned model predicts this error's distribution from history; the MPC adds it to the reduced model

**Right:** `fig01_task_target.png`

## 2. Error predictor pretrained on random function families
**Left**
- Causal Transformer over ~1 min history (states, commands, previous errors, wave elevations at 15 points); outputs error samples and safety quantiles
- Pretrained on ~10,000 random error laws simulated in the low-fidelity world, so it learns to recognise a law from history
- Three families: projection (around the reduced model), general (random filters + random networks), catalogue (~40 named physical mechanisms + small random layer)
- Where the prior's mass sits matters more than its width

**Right:** `fig02_predictor_priors.png`

## 3. Zero-shot accuracy and learning from target data
**Left**
- Test boat: high-fidelity planing model (48 strips, pump priming, roll), 16 two-minute missions
- Zero-shot skill (1 = no better than predicting zero): surge 0.11, sway 0.38, yaw 0.49, heave 0.05, pitch 0.35
- Catalogue beats the general and projection families zero-shot (sway 0.38 vs 0.58 vs 0.76)
- Fine-tuning a copy on 2 / 4 / 6 target missions cuts the error to 0.35 / 0.28 / 0.23 of the frozen network's: most of the remaining error is learnable

**Right:** `fig03_target_data.png`

## 4. Closed-loop results
**Left**
- Hand-tuned MPC 21.5 kn; reduced-model-only MPC 27.9 kn but ~300 impacts ≥ 7 g per hour
- MPC with the learned model: ~20 kn with fewer impacts; the learned safety quantities do act on the constraints
- No controller met the limits; in 85–97 % of planning steps all 128 plans violated a constraint
- Online learning: ~9 % one-step gain, mostly a static recalibration

**Right:** `fig04_closed_loop.png`

## 5. What we learned about the network
**Left**
- Wider network: ~10 % better, only with long history
- Memory structures: no gain; one window (~1 min) already identifies an in-family law
- Flow head: intervals collapse after fine-tuning; widening by scaling the input noise distorts mean and tails (a defect to fix)
- For one-step use, mean + variance is enough; the MPC needs calibrated tails of constraint quantities

**Right:** `fig05_flow_widening.png`

## 6. Is the high-fidelity boat inside the family?
**Left**
- In-family boats (small, median, large errors; "corner" boats with six effects all strong): fine-tuned copies find nothing to learn (≈ 1)
- Same test on the high-fidelity boat: 0.35, a large learnable part
- Train/test differences tested (sea synthesis, error-history noise, driving × sea on 16 family boats): none creates a learnable residual
- Wave direction: target-data gains shrink when directions vary (cause not yet separated)

**Right:** `fig06_inside_family.png`

## 7. Exact composition of the target's error
**Left**
- Wrote the full equations, replayed all 16 missions, split the error exactly into physical terms
- Sway: stern lateral force (∝ speed × stern immersion² × sideslip) vs constant damping, ~3/4 of the variance
- Yaw: difference of two large opposing moments; pitch: planing impact lift ~70 %; surge: four terms ~1/4 each
- The pump is often under-primed (hidden state); roll contributes exactly zero

**Right:** `fig07_error_composition.png`

---

## 8. Why the family cannot represent it
**Left**
- Same form, wrong place: lateral lift exists only at the bow
- Same form, values far outside the prior: heave–pitch coupling, pitch damping
- Different form: pump priming law, pressure drag
- 1,000 prior laws given the true states: best mixture still far off (sway 0.62 vs frozen 0.38 vs fine-tuned 0.07); sampling gives only an upper bound

**Right:** `fig08_family_gaps.png`

---

## 9. What the MPC actually needs
**Left**
- The MPC uses the model to rank plans by progress and to check constraint quantities
- Errors in progress cost little; errors in constraint quantities flip feasibility, so one-step MSE is the wrong yardstick
- Current bottleneck is how the constraints are written: the 3 g limit counts the past 60 s; the single-peak check is ~50× looser than the standard
- Only 1 of 64 closed-loop missions met 3 g; whether the limits are attainable in this sea is untested

**Right:** `fig09_mpc_needs.png`

## 10. Direction and decisions
**Left**
- General physics model: one cross-section force law summed along the hull covers the missing terms together; validate on data not produced by strip theory
- Wide prior + fine-tuning works with target data; large data needs coverage of conditions, data collected in rounds with the MPC in the loop, online calibration
- Next: (1) fix the constraints and measure the speed–impact boundary; (2) learn the constraint quantities over the horizon; (3) physics backbone; (4) real-boat sensors
- Decisions: allow checking experiments; reopen the physics grey-box route
